### Baseline

Most peers publish on a fixed weekday rhythm (weekly or several times a week) and keep a stable, recognisable section order from issue to issue. Typical issues run between about 700 and 2,500 words, and the subject is usually the title of the lead story or essay, often with a dek or "PLUS:" line naming the rest. Nearly all use light HTML with a few images and many inline links, and all twenty keep a public web archive. Signup pages are single-field email forms; half of them show a subscriber or reader count, and most name the publishing day or frequency.

### Gaps

- Only Seth's Blog and Jason Fried send near plain-text issues with no images and no links in the body; nobody else offers a plain-text edition.
- Only Social Media Watchblog publishes its schedule including the planned skipped dates; Robin Sloan and Hillel Wayne explain gaps inside the issue, everyone else stays silent about missed weeks.
- Only Pointer and The Rundown AI put a per-issue rating poll in the footer; only Ann Friedman prints reader replies back in the next issue ("FOLLOW-UP").
- Only Seth's Blog warns subscribers on the signup page about spam filters ("Google blocks many email subscriptions, even mine.") and only Social Media Watchblog's signup confirmation states the double opt-in step.
- No signup page shows the welcome email or says how to unsubscribe; only Pointer, Why Is This Interesting?, Lenny's Newsletter and Ann Friedman point to sample issues from the signup page ("Read a sample issue →", "Start here", "For a taste, check out the archive.").
- Only Why Is This Interesting? publishes an open rate ("43% lifetime average open rate"); no peer states its list-cleaning or inactivity policy.
