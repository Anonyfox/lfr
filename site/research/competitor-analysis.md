### Baseline

The market's page opens with a platform, ownership or category claim on a dark ground with white text and one bright accent, set in a neutral or custom grotesk sans, shows product UI screenshots, has no motion, asks for a sign-up or free trial, proves itself with feature lists, user counts or logos, prices a free tier that grows with subscribers (or is free open source you run yourself), speaks either in growth hype or plain technical terms, and gives the visitor nothing to take away before signing up.

### Gaps

- No peer shows the DNS records a domain needs, or how many there are, before sign-up.
- No peer mentions that cloud servers block outbound port 25, or how to send anyway.
- No peer shows the newsletter email a reader would actually receive.
- No peer shows an AI agent operating a newsletter from a terminal; Loops shows an agent only inside its own web app.
- No peer adds up the full monthly cost: a small server plus cents per thousand emails.
- No peer shows proof of authentication (a DKIM, SPF and DMARC pass) for mail it sends.
- No peer offers a copyable prompt or skill that hands the whole job to the visitor's own agent.
- No peer uses a monospace, terminal or postal visual language as its main idiom.
- No peer combines newsletters, list websites and personal mailboxes for many domains in one install.
